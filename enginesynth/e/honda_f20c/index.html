Engine Synthesizer

Sound is computed from the pressure waves travelling through a simulated engine's cylinders, intake and exhaust. No recordings.

Each engine is described by its dimensions: bore, stroke, valve timing, firing order and the lengths of its pipes. The simulation burns fuel in each cylinder, moves the gas through the valves and pipes, and what leaves the tailpipe and the intake is the sound you hear. It runs on your own device, tens of thousands of steps a second.

61 engines, 35 of them modelled on real ones from published specifications. Bore, stroke, firing order, power and torque are public; cam timing, valve sizes and pipe lengths rarely are, so those are estimated and tuned until each engine makes its published power and torque. Nobody has yet compared them by ear with the real engines, so treat it as a careful toy, not a measurement. Each engine's details in the library list what was estimated and link the sources.

Credits

The picture is drawn with three.js (MIT licence). The approach, making sound by simulating the physics rather than playing samples, was inspired by the Chimera vocal synthesizer.

What is simulated

The sound and everything that moves come from one physical model running in your browser: no recordings, no samples.

Simulated, and drawn from the simulation

  • Sound: pressure waves leaving the tailpipes and the intake, from gas flow solved through the cylinders, valves and pipes. One part is fitted, not simulated: above about 5 kHz the pipes lose treble by an amount matched to a slower, finer solver. Without it, pulses in the exhaust steepen into shocks that click.
  • Crank, pistons, valves: position and lift at true scale. Rotary engines: the housing bore, the eccentric shaft and the rotor's motion are exact; each of the three chambers glows with its own pressure and burn.
  • Gas colour in pipes: pressure relative to ambient (blue below, orange to white above; dark at ambient).
  • Header glow: temperature of the gas inside the exhaust pipes. The metal's own temperature is not simulated.
  • Cylinder glow and lamps: cylinder pressure, burn rate and gas temperature.
  • Sound on the floor: the floor of dots swells in slow pulses that spread from each tailpipe and intake opening. The pulses are slowed so they can be seen: their rate rises with engine speed (about one a second at idle, three at the redline) and their height follows the simulated loudness at each opening and the throttle. The colour around the engine is a spectrogram of the sound being played: pitch runs outward from the engine, time runs round it, and a line sweeps round once in nine seconds.
  • Tailpipe plume: its speed and density follow the gas velocity at the open end of the exhaust.
  • Dials: speed, torque, power, gear and road speed are the model's own numbers.

Decorative

  • Overrun flames: shown when the model cuts fuel at speed. Afterfire in the exhaust is simulated in the sound (Workshop, Fueling), but these flames are not drawn from it.
  • Camera shake, floor reflection, glow, vignette.
  • Rotor flank shape: a circular arc that stays just clear of the housing, standing in for the real flank and its combustion pocket.
  • Where the pipes run and what the parts look like: lengths and diameters are the simulated ones; the routing and shapes are a drawing. The picture is stroboscopic, so the engine appears to turn slowly.
